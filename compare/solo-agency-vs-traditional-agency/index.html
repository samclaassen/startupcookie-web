Solo Agency vs Traditional Agency: Which Fits a Startup?

By Barbara Jovanovic at StartupCookie | Last updated: November 2026
Honest comparison, limitations included

The short version: a solo agency, also called a one-person agency, is an agency where the founder does the client work herself instead of handing it to an account team. You get direct access to a senior person. You give up capacity: one person takes a limited number of clients and cannot run an enterprise-scale, multi-team program. StartupCookie is a solo agency. Founder Barbara Jovanovic runs every engagement, custom AI agents handle the production work, and the roster is capped at eight clients.

What a solo agency is

A solo agency is one person operating as an agency: one named senior operator who sells the work, plans it and produces it. People also call it a one-person agency, a one-person marketing agency or a solo marketing agency. The difference from a freelancer is mostly scope. A freelancer usually sells a deliverable. A solo agency runs a whole program, from strategy to distribution.

How one person ships a full program

The usual objection is volume. StartupCookie's answer is AI agents: custom agent pipelines handle research, drafting and repurposing, and Barbara spends her hours on strategy, editing and positioning. Onboarding takes two weeks from kickoff to first published asset, and founders give 1 to 2 hours of input per week.

Barbara walked the HubSpot team through the setup on Marketing Against the Grain, in "How I Run A 0-Employee Marketing Agency With AI Tools". It is the most-watched episode in the show's history, with 164k+ views. The episode is on the press page.

The results are public. StartupCookie has run Venn's content since August 2025, and co-founder Ahmed Shafik's LinkedIn closed 2025 at 1,192,605 impressions, up 405.2% on the prior year (Venn case study). Tofu's AI search visibility went from 7.00% to 67.38% within six months (Tofu case study).

Where a solo agency wins

  • Direct access. The person on the intro call does the work. At StartupCookie there are no account managers and no junior team.
  • Speed. Two weeks from kickoff to first published asset.
  • Entry price and terms. StartupCookie programs start at $5,000 a month, month-to-month.

Where a traditional agency wins

  • Scale. A large agency can absorb a six-figure, multi-workstream enterprise program that one person cannot.
  • Capacity. StartupCookie is one person with an eight-client cap, so there is sometimes a waitlist.
  • Range. StartupCookie does not run enterprise-scale multi-team programs, does not produce survey-based research reports, and works with B2B companies only.
  • Cover. A team has cover when someone is out. Ask any solo agency how it handles that before you sign.

Questions to ask a solo agency before you hire one

  1. Who does the work, and is anyone else involved?
  2. How many clients do you take at once?
  3. What do you not do?
  4. What is the minimum commitment?
  5. Which results are public, and where are the sources?

StartupCookie's answers: Barbara does the work, with custom AI agents handling production. Eight clients at most. The limits are listed above. Month-to-month. The case studies link every number to its source.

Solo agency or fractional CMO?

Some seed-stage teams need more than content. They need one person to own marketing decisions. For those teams Barbara works as a fractional CMO, the role she filled for Juno through January 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What is a solo agency?

A solo agency, also called a one-person agency, is an agency where the founder does the client work herself instead of handing it to an account team. It runs a whole program, from strategy to distribution, where a freelancer usually sells a single deliverable.

Is StartupCookie a solo agency?

Yes. StartupCookie is a one-person agency: founder Barbara Jovanovic runs every engagement herself, and custom AI agents handle the repeatable production work. There are no account managers and no junior team.

Can one person really run a full content program?

At StartupCookie, yes, within a cap of eight clients. Custom AI agent pipelines handle research, drafting and repurposing, and Barbara spends her hours on strategy, editing and positioning. Onboarding takes two weeks from kickoff to first published asset.

What are the risks of hiring a solo agency?

Capacity and scale. One person takes a limited number of clients, so there is sometimes a waitlist, and one person cannot run an enterprise-scale, multi-team program. Ask how many clients the agency takes and what it does not do.

How much does a solo agency cost?

It varies by agency. StartupCookie programs start at $5,000 a month, month-to-month, and full-stack programs run $10,000 to $15,000 a month.

Should a startup hire a solo agency or a bigger agency?

Pick a solo agency when you want direct access to a senior person and a program sized for a seed to Series B company. Pick a bigger agency when you need many workstreams at once or an enterprise-scale program.
